Supply Chain Analytics · Academic Team Case Study

Applichem Global Production Optimization

Evaluating how a global manufacturing network could balance cost efficiency, practical capacity, regional sourcing, and operational flexibility.

Excel Solver Optimization Scenario Analysis Supply Chain Strategy
Recommended scenario 80% Capacity + Strategic Routing
Total modeled cost $78.37M

01 · Business Problem

Cost alone was not enough.

The analysis evaluated production and distribution across a six-plant global network. The objective was to meet market demand while accounting for plant capacity, manufacturing economics, transportation, and the practical realities of operating the network.

A pure cost-minimizing solution could push facilities toward full utilization and create routing choices that were mathematically valid but less attractive operationally. The team therefore compared capacity scenarios and added strategic sourcing constraints.

02 · Approach

Model, stress-test, then improve the solution.

1

Build the optimization model

Use Excel Solver to allocate production flows while satisfying demand and plant-capacity constraints.

2

Test capacity assumptions

Compare a theoretical 100% capacity model with a more conservative 80% operating-capacity scenario.

3

Add business constraints

Modify selected routes to improve regional sourcing, diversification, and operational logic.

The revised routing shifted Mexico away from Japan and toward Venezuela, while Frankfurt supplied more of Japan. The team’s rationale emphasized regional sourcing, diversification, the shorter Mexico–Venezuela route, and Frankfurt’s efficiency for Japan.

05 · Recommendation

Choose the more resilient network.

The recommended solution was the modified 80% capacity model at approximately $78.37 million. It cost only about 0.12% more than the unconstrained 80% model, while producing a sourcing pattern the team considered more strategically balanced.

What This Project Demonstrates

Analytics that connects the model to the business.

This project demonstrates optimization modeling, scenario analysis, interpretation of capacity trade-offs, and the ability to move beyond a mathematically optimal answer toward a recommendation that considers operational flexibility and supply-chain strategy.
