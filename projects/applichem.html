Applichem Global Production Optimization

Using optimization and scenario analysis to balance cost, capacity, regional sourcing, and resilience across a six-plant manufacturing network.

01 · Business Problem

Cost alone was not enough.

The analysis evaluated production and distribution across a six-plant global network. The objective was to meet market demand while accounting for plant capacity, manufacturing economics, transportation, and the practical realities of operating the network.

A pure cost-minimizing solution could push facilities toward full utilization and create routing choices that were mathematically valid but less attractive operationally. The team therefore tested a more practical capacity assumption, added strategic sourcing constraints, and stress-tested the network under higher demand.

02 · Model Setup

Three assumptions shaped the analysis.

Demand 79.9M pounds

Demand was held constant to create a reliable baseline for comparing scenarios.

Capacity factor 80% of theoretical capacity

The team reduced the factor from 100% to allow room for maintenance, breakdowns, and unexpected disruptions.

Cost & yield Held constant

Production costs and plant yields were kept unchanged so plants could be compared consistently.

03 · Approach

Model, stress-test, then improve the solution.

05 · What-If Analysis

What happens if demand rises 10%?

The revised submission stress-tested the network by increasing demand to 87.9 million pounds. At an 80% capacity factor, the network no longer had enough effective capacity. Raising the capacity factor to 88% restored feasibility without adding a new plant.

The team concluded that moving from an 80% to an 88% capacity factor could absorb a 10% increase in demand while maintaining the existing plant network.

Why 3.4M lbs?

Mexico's original 3.4M-pound shipment to Japan was redirected to Venezuela instead of increasing Mexico's production.

How it connects

Mexico shifted 3.4M lbs from Japan to Venezuela, while Frankfurt shifted 3.4M lbs from Venezuela to Japan.

Business rationale

The change improved regional sourcing and diversification while reducing Venezuela's dependence on Frankfurt.

07 · Five-Year Recommendation

Give each plant a clearer strategic role.

The revised team recommendation was not to treat every plant the same. Instead, the network should use each location for the role that best matches its economics, capabilities, and local advantages.

FRA

Frankfurt

Primary global bulk hub

Leverage its lowest manufacturing cost, 98.9% yield, and strong technology; share best practices across the network.

GARY

Gary

U.S. customization

Specialize in U.S. customization while Frankfurt handles larger volumes of standardized product.

MEX

Mexico

Flexible regional hub

Use its capacity and workforce while transferring Frankfurt's process and waste-recovery practices.

CAN

Canada

Yield improvement

Improve yield, maintain high utilization, and continue serving Canadian and U.S. demand.

VEN

Venezuela

Focused domestic role

Repair the dryer, improve employee training, and keep its role primarily focused on domestic production.

SUN

Sunchem

Technical & support role

Focus on local packaging, quality control, engineering, and customer support while standardized bulk production shifts to Frankfurt.

What This Project Demonstrates

Analytics that connects the model to the business.

This project demonstrates optimization modeling, scenario analysis, capacity planning, interpretation of trade-offs, and the ability to move beyond a mathematically optimal answer toward a recommendation that considers operational flexibility and supply-chain strategy.
